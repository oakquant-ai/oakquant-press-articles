Why I wrote this: two reasons. First, I keep meeting smart people who can ship software but cannot cleanly explain the difference between an agent, a skill, and a tool, because the industry uses all three words interchangeably and badly. The employee analogy fixes that in one paragraph: the model is the talent, tools are the badge access, skills are the training. Once those land, the rest of the argument follows on its own.

Second, the most common question I get from architects right now is not whether to use agents, it is how many. We solved this problem once, in how we build companies, and forgot the lesson the moment the employees became software. Brooks worked out the math in 1975. Coase explained the economics in 1937. Neither of them ever saw a token bill, and both of them predicted it.
