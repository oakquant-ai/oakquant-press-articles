# Sausage, Poblano, and Mushroom Skillet with Broccolini

A familiar sausage-and-tomato dinner takes a different turn with poblano, king oyster mushrooms, and a restrained touch of ginger and Thai chili.

By Pumulo Sikaneta

## The idea

This recipe began with a practical question: what could be made from Italian sausage, one poblano pepper, broccolini, Thai chilis, garlic, onion, ginger, canned tomato, and king oyster mushrooms?

The answer was not to give every ingredient equal weight. Sausage and tomato form the center of the dish. The mushrooms are browned separately, the poblano cooks with the onion, and the broccolini goes in toward the end. Ginger and Thai chili are optional accents, not the foundation.

Serve it over rice, toss it with pasta, or bring the skillet to the table with crusty bread.

## Recipe at a glance

- Yield: 3-4 servings
- Time: approximately 40 minutes; allow longer if browning mushrooms in batches
- Equipment: a large, deep skillet with a lid and a plate for the browned mushrooms
- Heat level: adjustable
- Recipe status: a conversation-developed recipe, not independently kitchen-tested

## Ingredients

- 1 pound Italian sausage, casings removed
- 1 poblano pepper, stemmed, seeded, and sliced
- 1 bunch broccolini, trimmed and cut into approximately 2-inch pieces; keep thick stems separate from florets
- 2-3 king oyster mushrooms, cut into bite-size pieces or 1/4-inch-thick slices
- 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
- 3-4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 teaspoon finely grated ginger, optional
- 1/2-1 Thai chili, finely minced; use less or omit if your sausage is hot
- 1 can diced or crushed tomatoes, approximately 14-15 ounces, with their juices
- 1-2 tablespoons olive oil, as needed
- 1/4 cup water, plus more as needed
- Salt and black pepper, to taste
- Cooked rice, pasta, or crusty bread, for serving

## Method

### 1. Brown the mushrooms

Heat the skillet over medium-high heat. Add 1 tablespoon olive oil and the mushrooms in a single layer. Cook for 5-7 minutes, turning occasionally, until browned. Work in batches if necessary. Transfer to a plate.

### 2. Brown the sausage

Add the sausage to the same pan. Break it into bite-size chunks and cook for 6-8 minutes, until nicely browned. If it releases a lot of fat, spoon off the excess, leaving approximately 1 tablespoon in the pan.

### 3. Cook the onion and poblano

Add the onion and poblano to the sausage. Cook for 5-6 minutes, stirring occasionally. Lower the heat if the bottom of the pan begins to scorch.

### 4. Add the aromatics

Stir in the garlic, optional ginger, and Thai chili. Cook for approximately 30 seconds.

### 5. Build the sauce

Add the tomatoes and 1/4 cup water, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom of the skillet. Return the mushrooms to the pan. Simmer uncovered for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens.

### 6. Finish with broccolini

Stir in the thicker stem pieces first. Cover and cook for 3 minutes. Add the florets, cover again, and cook for another 2-4 minutes, until the broccolini reaches your preferred tenderness. Add a splash of water if the sauce becomes too dry. Make sure the sausage is cooked through; browning alone is not the final doneness check.

### 7. Taste and serve

Adjust the salt and black pepper. Spoon over rice, serve with bread, or toss with cooked pasta. For pasta, add a little pasta cooking water to loosen the sauce.

## Why this version is built this way

The recipe uses a clear hierarchy rather than treating the ingredient list as a checklist of equally strong flavors.

- Sausage and tomato anchor the skillet. Everything else is arranged around that combination.
- The mushrooms get their own browning step. This is intended to preserve a distinct browned component rather than cooking them entirely in tomato sauce.
- Onion and poblano share a cooking stage before the liquid goes in.
- Garlic, ginger, and chili enter shortly before the tomatoes, rather than spending the entire browning stage in the pan.
- Broccolini enters last, with the thicker stems given a head start. Its tenderness can then be adjusted without extending the cooking of the entire dish.
- Ginger is limited to a teaspoon. The aim is a small accent, not a ginger-led sauce.

These are the design intentions behind the recipe, not claims based on a separate test kitchen trial.

## Fusion inspiration, not a traditional origin story

This particular dish originated in an ingredient-led cooking conversation. It is not presented as an inherited regional recipe or as an established Italian, Mexican, or Thai dish.

Its Italian-inspired center is the combination of Italian sausage, tomato, garlic, and the option of serving it with pasta. Poblano adds a Mexican-associated ingredient to that framework. Thai chili and optional ginger provide another flavor direction, but their inclusion does not make the skillet a traditional Thai preparation.

The useful description is an Italian-inspired pantry fusion skillet. The fusion is in the ingredient choices; there is no documented historical lineage being claimed for the finished dish.

## Options

### The all-ingredient version

Use the ginger and begin with half a Thai chili. Keep the remaining chili aside until you have tasted the sauce. This is the version designed to accommodate the full original ingredient list.

### A more Italian-leaning version

Omit the ginger. Use a small amount of Thai chili simply as the hot-pepper element, or omit it as well. Serve with pasta or bread.

### A milder version

Use mild Italian sausage and leave out the Thai chili. The poblano remains part of the dish; this is a lower-heat option, not a guarantee of zero heat.

### A rice bowl

Keep the sauce thick enough to spoon over rice. This is the serving direction I would choose for the ginger-and-Thai-chili version.

### A pasta dinner

Toss the finished skillet mixture with cooked pasta, adding pasta cooking water a little at a time until the sauce coats it. The original recipe did not specify a dry pasta quantity, so choose the amount to suit your preferred sauce-to-pasta ratio.

## Practical notes

- Start conservatively with chili, especially when using hot sausage.
- Taste before adding much salt; the sausage is already seasoned.
- Use a wide pan, or brown the mushrooms in batches.
- Add water in small splashes rather than turning the skillet into a soup.
- The ingredient quantities describe the proposed recipe, not a record of the exact amounts originally available in the kitchen.
